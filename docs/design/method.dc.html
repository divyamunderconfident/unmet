Method & attribution

How the index is made, and whose words it quotes.

Nothing is hand-picked. A daily job reads public posts, a model sorts real needs from noise, and the ranking is arithmetic you can check.

01

Every day

  1. PullNew posts matching the need phrases are fetched and cached.
  2. ClassifyA free model reads them in batches of 20: is it a real need, what is the problem, how painful (1–3), would they pay?
  3. GroupLocal embeddings cluster similar needs into one problem, and the model names it.
  4. RankEach problem gets a score; the top 20 make the index.
  5. PublishResults are committed as JSON and the site rebuilds.
02

Sources

Hacker News through the official Algolia search API, and Stack Exchange through the official Stack Exchange API. No scraping, and no Reddit or X, whose API terms rule them out. We read the last 30 days of posts that contain:

I wish there wasis there a toollooking for a way towould pay forwe gave up onwhy is there nodoes anyone know a toolI'd love an app
03

How ranking works

People× Recency× Pain+ Pay boost

People counts different authors, not posts. Recency gives newer posts more weight. Pain is the 1–3 level the classifier assigns. A post saying they would pay lifts the score further.

06

Attribution & licence

Every quote shows its author's name, its source and a link to the original post. We keep short excerpts only, never full posts; a cut is marked with “…”.

Content from Stack Exchange sites is licensed under Creative Commons BY-SA, and each Stack Exchange quote credits its author with links to their profile and the original post. Hacker News comments remain their authors' own.

Remove a post

Wrote one of the quoted posts and want it off Unmet? Open an issue with the link to the original post, and it will be dropped from the next daily edition and from the stored data.
